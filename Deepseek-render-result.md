In the morning, Esmeralda, the girl's mother, gave a basket to Maria, the girl in the red hood. The basket of food was passed from mother to daughter at the edge of the dark woods, the girl's hands closing around the handle as she set out. Esmeralda warned Maria, "Stay on the path." The words hung in the morning air as Maria nodded and turned toward the trees.

Maria, in the dark woods, met Grumple, the hungry wolf. The trees closed overhead, and the path narrowed to a ribbon of dirt between roots. Grumple asked Maria, "Where are you going?" The wolf's voice came low and casual from the shadow of a trunk. Maria answered Grumple, "To grandmother's cottage." She kept her eyes on the path ahead as she spoke.

Grumple, at grandmother's cottage, swallowed Monique, the sick grandmother. The cottage stood quiet among the trees, its door unlatched, and the wolf slipped inside without a sound. Grumple, in the bed, disguised as grandmother. The wolf pulled the blankets up to his chin and arranged the grandmother's nightcap over his ears. In the afternoon, at grandmother's cottage, Maria said, "What big eyes you have." She stood at the foot of the bed, basket still in hand, studying the figure under the covers. Gary, the passing woodcutter, at grandmother's cottage, cut the wolf open. The woodcutter's blade parted the wolf's belly in one clean stroke, and the seams of the disguise fell away. Monique, alive, emerged. The grandmother drew her first free breath and sat up in the ruins of the bedding.
